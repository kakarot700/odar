Global average temperature has risen by about **1.09°C** for the 2011–2020 decade compared with the pre-industrial baseline (1850–1900), according to the IPCC’s Sixth Assessment Report [1]. More recent single years have been warmer still: 2023 was about **1.45°C** above pre-industrial levels, though this reflects short-term variability on top of the long-term trend [2].

The main driver is **human activity**. IPCC assessment finds that human-caused warming contributed roughly **1.07°C** of the observed total [1]. This warming is dominated by increases in **well-mixed greenhouse gases**—especially carbon dioxide (CO₂), methane (CH₄), and nitrous oxide (N₂O)—emitted from fossil fuel combustion, deforestation, agriculture, and industrial processes. Other anthropogenic factors, such as aerosols, exert a net cooling effect that partially masks some greenhouse warming, while natural drivers (solar and volcanic) contribute only a small, near-zero amount [1]. In summary, the long-term rise is essentially due to greenhouse gas emissions, with CO₂ being the largest single contributor.
