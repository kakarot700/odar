Intermittent fasting (IF) is **not inherently better** than daily calorie restriction (CR) for weight loss. When total calorie intake is matched, both approaches produce similar results.

A 12-month randomized trial comparing alternate-day fasting with daily CR found no significant difference in weight loss, with participants in both groups losing around 5–6% of body weight [1]. Likewise, a study on time-restricted eating (8-hour eating window) showed weight loss comparable to daily CR among adults with obesity [2].

The primary driver of weight loss is a sustained energy deficit, not meal timing. IF often works because it helps individuals reduce total intake automatically, but daily CR can achieve the same effect through portion control. Adherence is the strongest predictor of success—some people find IF easier to follow, while others prefer flexible daily tracking [1][2].

Practical differences exist: IF may cause short-term side effects like hunger, headaches, or fatigue, and can be harder to maintain in social settings. Daily CR allows more frequent eating but requires ongoing calorie counting. Neither method consistently outperforms the other in long-term trials, so the best choice depends on personal lifestyle and sustainability [2]. Experts recommend selecting a strategy you can maintain rather than chasing superior meal timing.
