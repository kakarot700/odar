# COP30 in Belém, Brazil (November 2025): Main Outcomes and Climate Agreements

## Executive Summary

The 30th Conference of the Parties to the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (COP30) convened in Belém, Brazil, from 10 to 21 November 2025, with the closing plenary ultimately held on 22 November 2025 after negotiations ran into overtime ([European Parliament](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/ATAG/2025/779230/EPRS_ATA(2025)779230_EN.pdf); [World Resources Institute [WRI]](https://www.wri.org/insights/cop30-outcomes-next-steps)). Nearly 200 countries gathered amid intensifying climate impacts and difficult geopolitical conditions, including the United States' January 2025 decision to withdraw from the Paris Agreement ([European Parliament](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/ATAG/2025/779230/EPRS_ATA(2025)779230_EN.pdf)). The summit adopted the **Belém Political Package**, anchored by the so-called "Global Mutirão" decision—a term drawn from the Brazilian tradition of communal cooperation—and delivered significant breakthroughs on climate finance, adaptation support, Indigenous Peoples' rights, and forest protection, while falling short of the binding fossil fuel phase-out commitment that many negotiators had sought ([UN Global Compact Network Italia [UNGCN Italy]](https://www.globalcompactnetwork.org/en/news/ungcn-italy-news/2639-cop30-in-belem-main-outcomes-and-climate-agreements.html); [Center for Climate and Energy Solutions [C2ES]](https://www.c2es.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/COP30-Summary-FINAL5.pdf)).

This report synthesizes the confirmed outcomes of COP30, evaluates the scope and significance of the main decisions, and offers a balanced assessment of what the summit achieved and what it left unresolved.

---

## Context: A Summit Under Pressure

COP30 opened under unusually challenging circumstances. Geopolitical fragmentation, rising global greenhouse gas emissions, and the continued expansion of fossil fuel infrastructure cast a shadow over the proceedings. The United States' withdrawal from the Paris Agreement—announced in January 2025 and leaving it as the sole non-party among major emitters—significantly weakened the multilateral framework that had underpinned the previous decade of climate diplomacy ([European Parliament](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/ATAG/2025/779230/EPRS_ATA(2025)779230_EN.pdf)).

In this context, the Brazilian Presidency framed COP30 as an opportunity to demonstrate that multilateralism can still deliver. As reported by the United Nations, "A new economy is rising, while the old polluting one is running out of road," a sentiment echoed by world leaders at the World Climate Leaders Summit held on 6–7 November 2025, which set the tone for the negotiations and emphasized the enduring relevance of the Paris Agreement ([de Carvalho](https://news.un.org/en/story/2025/11/1166433); [C2ES](https://www.c2es.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/COP30-Summary-FINAL5.pdf)).

---

## The Belém Political Package and the "Global Mutirão" Decision

The central deliverable of COP30 was the **Global Mutirão: Uniting humanity in a global mobilization against climate change**, the formal decision adopted on 22 November 2025 that formed the core of the Belém Political Package ([C2ES](https://www.c2es.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/COP30-Summary-FINAL5.pdf); [European Parliament](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/ATAG/2025/779230/EPRS_ATA(2025)779230_EN.pdf)). The name reflects the Brazilian concept of *mutirão*—a tradition of collective effort rooted in community cooperation—and the text was crafted through two weeks of intensive Presidency consultations with Parties to convey the spirit of global solidarity ([C2ES](https://www.c2es.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/COP30-Summary-FINAL5.pdf)).

The decision was greeted with applause in the final plenary, but critical observers considered it insufficiently incisive, particularly with respect to energy and mitigation policies. Notably, it reaffirmed multilateral cooperation and continuity with the Paris Agreement's objectives **without introducing binding commitments on the phase-out of fossil fuels** ([UNGCN Italy](https://www.globalcompactnetwork.org/en/news/ungcn-italy-news/2639-cop30-in-belem-main-outcomes-and-climate-agreements.html)). The European Parliament's briefing confirmed that "while the EU and many other Parties had called for a COP decision on a roadmap to transition away from fossil fuels, this was blocked by a group of mostly oil- and gas-producing countries" ([European Parliament](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/ATAG/2025/779230/EPRS_ATA(2025)779230_EN.pdf)).

The decision also marks the tenth anniversary of the Paris Agreement, celebrating its achievements while launching several new implementation mechanisms discussed below ([C2ES](https://www.c2es.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/COP30-Summary-FINAL5.pdf)).

## Climate Finance: The Summit's Strongest Achievement

Climate finance emerged as COP30's most substantive outcome. The adopted text calls for **mobilizing at least $1.3 trillion per year by 2035** for climate action, building directly on the climate finance goal agreed at COP29 in Baku ([de Carvalho](https://news.un.org/en/story/2025/11/1166433)). The World Resources Institute described the resulting **"Baku to Belém Roadmap to $1.3 Trillion"** as "a step change by outlining practical steps to ensure different sources of finance are working together to drive investment at scale" ([WRI](https://www.wri.org/news/statement-cop30-delivers-forests-and-finance-underdelivers-fossil-fuels)).

Several specific financial commitments and mechanisms were announced:

| Commitment / Mechanism | Description | Source |
|---|---|---|
| $1.3 trillion per year by 2035 | Minimum annual climate finance mobilization target | [de Carvalho, UN News](https://news.un.org/en/story/2025/11/1166433) |
| Tripling of adaptation finance | Explicit call in the final text to triple support for climate adaptation | [de Carvalho](https://news.un.org/en/story/2025/11/1166433); [European Parliament](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/ATAG/2025/779230/EPRS_ATA(2025)779230_EN.pdf) |
| Loss and damage fund operationalization | Agreement to operationalize the fund established at COP28 | [de Carvalho](https://news.un.org/en/story/2025/11/1166433) |
| Baku to Belém Roadmap to $1.3 Trillion | Practical steps for mobilizing diverse financial sources at scale | [WRI](https://www.wri.org/news/statement-cop30-delivers-forests-and-finance-underdelivers-fossil-fuels) |
| Risk guarantees and country platforms | Announced instruments moving finance concepts toward implementation | [WRI](https://www.wri.org/news/statement-cop30-delivers-forests-and-finance-underdelivers-fossil-fuels) |

UNGCN Italy summarized the overall character of the outcome as "climate finance and adaptation: a concrete commitment" ([UNGCN Italy](https://www.globalcompactnetwork.org/en/news/ungcn-italy-news/2639-cop30-in-belem-main-outcomes-and-climate-agreements.html)), while the European Parliament noted that "key outcomes include new initiatives to support implementation and ambition, a set of indicators for climate adaptation, and a call for the tripling of adaptation finance" ([European Parliament](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/ATAG/2025/779230/EPRS_ATA(2025)779230_EN.pdf)).

## New Initiatives for Implementation and Ambition

COP30 launched several new initiatives designed to help countries translate climate commitments into action on the ground:

### Global Implementation Accelerator
This initiative was created to help countries deliver on their nationally determined contributions (NDCs) and climate adaptation plans, providing technical and operational support to accelerate the pace of implementation ([de Carvalho](https://news.un.org/en/story/2025/11/1166433); [European Parliament](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/ATAG/2025/779230/EPRS_ATA(2025)779230_EN.pdf)).

### Belém Mission to 1.5°C
Named after the host city, this mission was established to raise ambition and support implementation of climate action consistent with the Paris Agreement's 1.5°C temperature goal ([de Carvalho](https://news.un.org/en/story/2025/11/1166433); [European Parliament](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/ATAG/2025/779230/EPRS_ATA(2025)779230_EN.pdf)). While the summit avoided a binding phase-out commitment, this initiative represents an attempt to keep the 1.5°C objective operationally alive through national action plans rather than through a multilateral mandate.

### Adaptation Indicators and Information Integrity
The package also includes **a set of indicators for climate adaptation**, providing a framework for measuring progress on adaptation efforts ([European Parliament](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/ATAG/2025/779230/EPRS_ATA(2025)779230_EN.pdf)). Additionally, the text includes a **commitment to promoting information integrity on climate change**, an increasingly relevant issue given the spread of climate misinformation ([European Parliament](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/ATAG/2025/779230/EPRS_ATA(2025)779230_EN.pdf)).

## Indigenous Peoples, Equity, and the Global Ethical Stocktake

One of COP30's most widely praised dimensions was its treatment of equity and human rights. WRI noted that "COP30 succeeded in putting people at the center of climate action," highlighting that Indigenous Peoples participated in record numbers and that the **Global Ethical Stocktake** affirmed that "fairness, inclusion, and responsibility must guide every decision" ([WRI](https://www.wri.org/news/statement-cop30-delivers-forests-and-finance-underdelivers-fossil-fuels)). New commitments for Indigenous Peoples' and communities' land rights and climate finance were characterized as "a strong step forward," though WRI cautioned that "far more is needed" ([WRI](https://www.wri.org/news/statement-cop30-delivers-forests-and-finance-underdelivers-fossil-fuels)).

The WRI headline statement for COP30 encapsulated the summit's mixed legacy succinctly: **"COP30 Delivers on Forests and Finance, Underdelivers on Fossil Fuels"** ([WRI](https://www.wri.org/news/statement-cop30-delivers-forests-and-finance-underdelivers-fossil-fuels)).

---

## Thematic Dialogue on Mountains and Climate Change

The COP30 decision also requests a **dialogue on mountains and climate change to be held in June 2026**, at which Parties will determine whether to host annual dialogues in the future. A summary report of the dialogue is to be presented at COP31 in conjunction with the launch of the second Global Stocktake (GST) cycle ([C2ES](https://www.c2es.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/COP30-Summary-FINAL5.pdf)).

---

## The Fossil Fuel Question: A Central Gap

Perhaps the most discussed shortcoming of COP30 was the failure to secure a binding decision on transitioning away from fossil fuels. The summit issued only a **pledge to plan a fossil fuel transition**, without concrete targets, timelines, or enforcement mechanisms ([de Carvalho](https://news.un.org/en/story/2025/11/1166433)). The European Parliament delegation chair, Lídia Pereira (EPP, Portugal), regretted that "the outcome did not match the EU's ambition," remarking that "while momentum for global climate action is slower than it should be, multilateralism held" ([European Parliament](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/ATAG/2025/779230/EPRS_ATA(2025)779230_EN.pdf)). The European Parliament had explicitly called for "a stronger commitment to transition away from fossil fuels" and "concrete plans to phase out fossil fuel subsidies" in its pre-conference resolution ([European Parliament](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/ATAG/2025/779230/EPRS_ATA(2025)779230_EN.pdf)).

The gap between COP30 outcomes and scientific recommendations was underscored by Brazilian scientist Carlos Nobre, who warned before the final plenary that **fossil fuel use must fall to zero by 2040–2045 at the latest** to avoid catastrophic temperature rises of up to 2.5°C by mid-century—outcomes that would mean the near-total loss of coral reefs, the collapse of the Amazon rainforest, and accelerated melting of the Greenland ice sheet ([de Carvalho](https://news.un.org/en/story/2025/11/1166433)).

## Assessment and Outlook

### What COP30 Achieved
COP30 delivered on several fronts: a significant and concrete climate finance commitment anchored to the $1.3 trillion annual figure; operationalization pathways for the loss and damage fund; tripling of adaptation finance; two implementation-oriented initiatives (the Global Implementation Accelerator and the Belém Mission to 1.5°C); Indigenous Peoples' rights and equity provisions; adaptation indicators; and renewed affirmation of multilateralism in the face of the United States' withdrawal from the Paris Agreement ([de Carvalho](https://news.un.org/en/story/2025/11/1166433); [C2ES](https://www.c2es.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/COP30-Summary-FINAL5.pdf); [WRI](https://www.wri.org/news/statement-cop30-delivers-forests-and-finance-underdelivers-fossil-fuels)).

### What COP30 Left Unresolved
The summit did not deliver a binding fossil fuel phase-out roadmap, nor did it impose targets on oil- and gas-producing countries—the very countries that blocked such a decision ([European Parliament](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/RegData/etudes/ATAG/2025/779230/EPRS_ATA(2025)779230_EN.pdf)). WRI experts acknowledged that while the resulting decision secured "some important wins, both inside and outside the negotiations," it "omitted some of the big-ticket items many hoped to see" ([WRI](https://www.wri.org/insights/cop30-outcomes-next-steps)). UNGCN Italy similarly noted that the Mutirão Decision was met with applause but was "considered insufficiently incisive due to gaps in energy and mitigation policies" ([UNGCN Italy](https://www.globalcompactnetwork.org/en/news/ungcn-italy-news/2639-cop30-in-belem-main-outcomes-and-climate-agreements.html)).

### Overall Interpretation
In my assessment, COP30 represents a **pragmatic, finance-centered multilateralism** that advanced implementation infrastructure while deferring the most politically contentious mitigation decisions. The summit's architecture—prioritizing national action through NDC delivery mechanisms rather than collective phase-out mandates—effectively relocates the fossil fuel question from the multilateral negotiating table to the national policy arena, where progress will depend on domestic political conditions rather than international pressure. The $1.3 trillion annual finance commitment is significant in principle, but its credibility will hinge on the political will of developed economies and on whether the "Baku to Belém Roadmap" translates concepts into disbursed funds. Meanwhile, the scientific trajectory described by Carlos Nobre suggests that without more decisive mitigation action, the 1.5°C goal remains increasingly unattainable, regardless of the implementation innovations launched at Belém.
