# Research Report: Does raising the minimum wage reduce employment? Summarize what the empirical evidence says, including where economists disagree.

## Answer (written only from the certified findings below)
Some economists are willing to admit uncertainty about what a $15 minimum wage would do to US employment and productivity [[1]](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/what-economists-think-about-a-15-minimum-wage), even as surveys have found that nearly three-quarters of economists oppose a $15 federal minimum wage and roughly 74% say the policy is ineffective at lifting workers out of poverty, with a majority preferring other anti-poverty tools [[3]](https://epionline.org/studies/data-snapshot-three-quarters-of-economists-oppose-15-minimum-wage-proposals/)[[3]](https://epionline.org/studies/data-snapshot-three-quarters-of-economists-oppose-15-minimum-wage-proposals/).

The Congressional Budget Office's median estimate is that 1.3 million workers would become jobless in an average week in 2025 [[2]](https://www.factcheck.org/2021/01/paul-distorts-cbos-estimate-on-impact-of-15-minimum-wage/), and Moody's Analytics averages an estimated 1.4 million job losses in 2025 [[2]](https://www.factcheck.org/2021/01/paul-distorts-cbos-estimate-on-impact-of-15-minimum-wage/), while some politicians have cited figures close to 4 million based on government estimates [[2]](https://www.factcheck.org/2021/01/paul-distorts-cbos-estimate-on-impact-of-15-minimum-wage/). Steve Kaplan of Chicago Booth strongly agreed that the higher wage would raise the cost of entry-level and low-wage jobs and thus adversely affect the unemployment rate [[1]](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/what-economists-think-about-a-15-minimum-wage), and most economists surveyed expect the elimination of the federal tip credit to reduce tipped jobs in full-service restaurants [[3]](https://epionline.org/studies/data-snapshot-three-quarters-of-economists-oppose-15-minimum-wage-proposals/).

A classic comparison of New Jersey and Pennsylvania fast-food stores after New Jersey raised its minimum wage found that stores unaffected by the hike grew employment at the same rate as Pennsylvania stores, while stores that had to raise wages actually increased employment, challenging the prediction that higher minimum wages reduce employment [[4]](https://www.nber.org/papers/w4509)[[2]](https://www.factcheck.org/2021/01/paul-distorts-cbos-estimate-on-impact-of-15-minimum-wage/)[[4]](https://www.nber.org/papers/w4509)[[4]](https://www.nber.org/papers/w4509). The disagreement is therefore about the sign of the employment effect and its magnitude, not merely about the policy's desirability [[1]](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/what-economists-think-about-a-15-minimum-wage)[[1]](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/what-economists-think-about-a-15-minimum-wage).
