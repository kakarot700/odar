The main findings were:

Malaria control prevented an estimated 2.2 billion cases and 12.7 million deaths since 2000.
In 2023, malaria still caused about 263 million cases and 597,000 deaths worldwide, with cases up by about 11 million from 2022 and deaths staying about the same.
The burden remained heavily concentrated in Africa: about 95% of malaria deaths occurred in the WHO African Region.
Progress is uneven: many countries are moving toward elimination, but the highest-burden countries continue to account for most cases and deaths.
The report added a new focus on inequity, highlighting that children under 5, women and girls, Indigenous Peoples, migrants, people with disabilities, and people in remote areas are especially vulnerable.
The big message was that better access to prevention, diagnosis, treatment, and surveillance is needed to reverse stalled progress and reduce inequities.
