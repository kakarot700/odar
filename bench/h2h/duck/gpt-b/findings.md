"Recent studies suggest microplastics may affect several human systems, but the strongest evidence is still limited and much of it comes from animal or observational research. The main concerns are reproductive, digestive, respiratory, and possibly cardiovascular health. A 2024 rapid systematic review concluded microplastic exposure is suspected to harm reproductive, digestive, and respiratory health, while evidence for birth outcomes and gestational age was not strong enough to classify. [citation chip: NRDC +1]

More specifically, recent human and lab studies have found microplastics in blood, lungs, placenta, liver, and arterial plaque, and associations with inflammation, oxidative stress, impaired sperm quality, and possible fetal growth effects. Some newer cardiovascular studies also found higher rates of heart attack, stroke, or death among people whose arterial plaque contained micro- or nanoplastics, but those studies show association, not proof of causation. [citation chip: NRDC +3]

So the short version is: likely harms are being taken seriously, especially for reproduction, gut, and lungs, but we still don’t have definitive evidence that everyday exposure directly causes specific diseases in humans."

## Q9 — EU AI Act provisions and effective dates

Web search: ON
Chat title: "EU AI Act provisions and effective dates"
Status: "Response complete"

Answer text (verbatim):
"The EU AI Act uses a risk-based approach:

Unacceptable-risk AI is banned, including certain manipulative systems, social scoring, and some biometric uses.
High-risk AI has the toughest compliance duties, such as risk management, data governance, documentation, transparency, human oversight, and accuracy/cybersecurity controls.
Limited-risk AI mainly has transparency duties, like telling users when they're interacting with AI or when content is AI-generated.
General-purpose AI (GPAI) models have their own rules for documentation, transparency, and copyright compliance.
Lower-risk AI is mostly unregulated beyond existing law and voluntary practices. [citation chip: European Union +1]

Timing-wise, the Act entered into force on 1 August 2024 and applies in phases: bans and AI literacy rules started on 2 February 2025; GPAI governance rules started on 2 August 2025; the main transparency and GPAI enforcement provisions started on 2 August 2026; and the high-risk rules now apply later for most systems — 2 December 2027 for certain stand-alone high-risk uses and 2 August 2028 for high-risk AI built into regulated products.
